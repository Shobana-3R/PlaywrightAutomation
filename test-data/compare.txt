C. Defect management
1. Which tool is used to log defects?
2. What information is mandatory when creating a defect?
3. How are severity and priority defined?
4. Who assigns defects to developers?
5. What are the agreed turnaround times for critical, high and medium defects?
6. How are defects linked to user stories, test cases and releases?
7. What are the defect lifecycle statuses?
8. Who decides whether a defect is a blocker for release?
9. How are duplicate defects and rejected defects handled?
10. How is retesting performed after a fix?
11. Do we need to run regression for every defect fix?
12. Are defect triage meetings conducted daily or at another frequency?
13. Is there a backlog of open defects that the incoming QA engineers need to take over?
14. Do you track defect leakage, reopened defects, production incidents or root causes?

D. Test environments and test data
1. What environments are available — development, QA, integration, staging and UAT?
2. Which environment should our QA team use for daily testing?
3. How often are builds deployed to the QA environment?
4. Who manages deployments and environment availability?
5. Are environment refreshes scheduled?
6. Do environments have the same configuration as production?
7. Are there dependencies on other services, APIs or third-party systems?
8. How is test data created, maintained, reset and cleaned up?
9. Is synthetic or masked data available for testing?
10. Are there reusable datasets for different business scenarios?
11. Are there restrictions on creating or deleting test cases, users or reports?
12. What is the process when an environment is down or test data becomes unusable?
13. Are there known differences between local, QA and cloud execution environments?
14. Are service virtualization, mocks or stubs available when dependencies are unavailable?
Because EthicsPoint handles sensitive reports, ask specifically about approved test-data practices, role-based access and restrictions on using production data. Do not assume that real reporter details or real case information can be used for testing.


6. API, integrations and non-functional testing
These questions help uncover testing responsibilities that may not be obvious from the UI demo.
API and integration testing
- Are APIs documented in Swagger/OpenAPI, Postman collections or another tool?
- Does the team perform API testing independently of UI testing?
- Which services and external systems does EthicsPoint integrate with?
- Are API contracts and schemas available?
- How are authentication, authorization and token expiry tested?
- How are API errors, timeouts, retries and duplicate requests handled?
- Are there integration environments or mocks for dependent services?
- Who owns integration testing when an API is developed by another Scrum team?
- Are database validations permitted, and what access is available?
- How do we verify that UI changes are correctly persisted and reflected in downstream systems?

Non-functional testing
- Who owns performance, load and stress testing?
- Are there defined response-time or throughput targets?
- Is security testing handled by a dedicated security team?
- Are accessibility and cross-browser compatibility in scope?
- How are authorization boundaries and sensitive-data exposure tested?
- Are audit logs and data-retention rules part of QA validation?
- Are disaster recovery, resilience and availability scenarios tested?
- Are there compliance requirements that influence the testing process?
You do not need to take ownership of all these areas. The goal is to understand what your QA team is expected to cover and what specialist teams handle.
